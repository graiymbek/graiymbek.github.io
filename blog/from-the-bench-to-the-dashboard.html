How My Journey Into Data Analytics Actually Started

It didn't start with a certification or a bootcamp. It started with a professor asking if I knew Linux, three months to learn Bash and Python from scratch, and a dataset of cancer mutations I couldn't put down.

October 7, 20264 min read

Years ago, I joined a computational biology lab as a volunteer because I wanted to learn computational skills and bioinformatics. At the time I had a strong biology background — but almost no computational experience at all.

During my first meeting, my professor asked me a question that quietly changed the direction of my career, even though neither of us knew it yet:

"Do you know Linux?"

My honest answer was something close to, "There's another operating system besides Windows?" He gave me three months to learn Linux, Bash, and Python before he'd even assign me a project. So that's what I did.

The project that hooked me

After those first three months, I started working on a project involving large biological datasets pulled from several public scientific databases. The goal was to prepare the data for a machine-learning project that would distinguish cancer-driver mutations from non-driver mutations.

And this is where something shifted for me.

I had huge CSV files from different sources that needed to be combined, cleaned, transformed, and organized before any of it could be used for analysis. At first I did most of the transformation in Bash. Then I discovered Python, pandas, and NumPy — and suddenly I understood how much I enjoyed this part of the work.

Taking large, messy datasets and turning them into something structured and meaningful was genuinely exciting to me.

That was where my interest in data analytics actually began — years before I ever called myself a data analyst.

Back to the bench

I eventually had to move back into wet-lab research and never got to finish the machine-learning part of that project. But the interest in working with data didn't go anywhere. It just waited.

Coming back, this time on purpose

About six years later, I decided to come back to it — intentionally this time. I studied data analytics, strengthened my SQL and Power BI skills, earned my Microsoft Power BI certification, and eventually transitioned from scientific research into working professionally with data and business intelligence.

Today I work with Power BI, SQL, data modeling, DAX, and business data day to day, and I'm continuing to expand into areas like Microsoft Fabric and data science.

Where the two worlds might meet

I haven't forgotten where I came from. One of my long-term goals is to eventually bring my two worlds together — biotechnology and data. With a research background on one side and growing analytics experience on the other, I'd love to work with life-science or biotechnology data again someday, but from a completely different perspective: not as the person generating the data at the bench, but as the person turning it into something decision-makers can actually use.

Looking back, I don't think my career change was really starting over. I think I was simply coming back to something I discovered years ago — with far more experience than I had the first time.
